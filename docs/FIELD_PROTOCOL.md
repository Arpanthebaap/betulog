# Suggested field and lab protocol (draft)

This is a starting point to adapt with your supervisors and the project's own standards, not an official method.

## 1. Plot design
- Choose plots to contrast healthy and declining parts of a stand; record stand type, age, site and management history
- Mark plot corners; measure each tree's X/Y from one corner and record DBH
- Keep the same trees across visits; do not renumber

## 2. Crown scoring
- Score from two sides at a distance of about one tree height, in similar light, outside leaf flush and autumn colour
- Score defoliation against a reference tree for the site, in 5% steps, and record dieback and discolouration separately
- Calibrate observers: all score the same 20 trees, compare, and repeat until differences are small; report the agreement
- Record the date and phenological stage with every assessment

## 3. Symptoms
Tick only what you can see. Photograph each symptom with a scale bar and note the tree ID in the file name. Record browsing intensity and height for young plantations.

## 4. Sampling for isolation
- Take samples with clean, flame-sterilised or disposable tools; one tool per tree or surface-sterilise between trees
- Sample the margin between healthy and symptomatic tissue, not dead centre
- Label bag and Betulog entry with the same isolate ID and tree ID; keep samples cool and process quickly
- Record tissue, date, medium, and any contamination

## 5. Lab chain of custody
Update the isolate status at each step (plated, pure culture, DNA extracted, sequenced, identified). Deposit sequences in a public database and put the accession in the log. Keep cultures in a collection with the isolate ID.

## 6. Analysis
Treat trees as nested in plots and sites. Report scoring method, observer calibration and sample sizes. Test associations with mixed models; do not infer causation from symptom co-occurrence alone.
